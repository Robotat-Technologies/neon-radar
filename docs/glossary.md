# Glossary

The terms NeonRadar's documentation, typology files and interface use, with their sources. Definitions are paraphrases unless they are in quotation marks. Each citation is a key from [the bibliography](sources/bibliography.yaml); [sources/README.md](sources/README.md) explains how to cite. Terms marked "project" are NeonRadar's own and need no external source.

Three rules follow from these definitions:

- **Beneficial owner** means a natural person only. A company that owns another company is a legal or intermediate owner.
- **STR and SAR**: NeonRadar treats them as the same kind of report (project terminology). Write STR for FATF and non-US contexts and SAR for US material.
- **Nominee** is the word to use. "Strawman" and "proxy" are synonyms (project terminology).

A term in this glossary describes a structure or a pattern. Using it about a structure in the data never says that a person or company did anything wrong ([docs/wording.md](wording.md)).

| Term | Definition | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| Placement, layering, integration | Three stages used to describe money laundering: placement introduces illicit proceeds into the financial system; layering moves them through "a series of conversions or movements of the funds to distance them from their source" [fatf_faq]; integration returns them to the legitimate economy. Europol uses the same three stages [europol_efecta_2023] and calls this "a simplified representation of a more complex reality" and notes that digitalisation is making the steps "much more blurred" [europol_efecta_2023]. | [fatf_faq] [europol_efecta_2023] |
| Beneficial owner | The natural person or persons who ultimately own or control a customer, or on whose behalf a transaction is conducted, including those who exercise ultimate effective control over a legal person or arrangement. Only natural persons are beneficial owners; a company that owns a company is a legal or intermediate owner (project terminology). | [fatf_glossary] |
| Legal owner, intermediate owner | Companies and arrangements in an ownership chain between the subject and its beneficial owners. | project |
| Control | Ownership is one route to control. The Puppet Masters study argues that beneficial ownership should be understood as de facto control, which "can be exercised in many different ways, including through ownership, contractually or informally" [star_puppet_masters_2011]. Where the law defines beneficial ownership by control as well as ownership, a person can be a beneficial owner even when their ownership falls below the threshold [openownership_thresholds]. | [star_puppet_masters_2011] [openownership_thresholds] |
| 25 percent threshold | An illustrative FATF example that became a legal threshold in the EU and elsewhere; it is not a FATF requirement. Open Ownership notes that FATF "does not recommend a specific threshold level, but mentions a 25% figure in the context of examples" [openownership_thresholds]; the EU wrote it into Directive (EU) 2015/849, Article 3(6) [eba_qa_2022_6622]. Several countries use lower thresholds, and control can exist below any threshold [openownership_thresholds]. The example is in the Interpretive Note to Recommendation 10 [fatf_recommendations_2026], checked in the 2012 text as reproduced by the Tax Justice Network (2016); not re-checked against the June 2026 edition (FATF blocks automated access). | [fatf_recommendations_2026] [openownership_thresholds] [eba_qa_2022_6622] |
| Politically exposed person (PEP) | A person who is or has been entrusted with a prominent public function by a country (a foreign or domestic PEP) or by an international organisation; not middle-ranking or more junior officials. The FATF glossary text was last read on 16 October 2026 from the glossary index page; not re-checked since (FATF blocks automated access). | [fatf_glossary] [fatf_peps_2013] |
| RCA | NeonRadar's shorthand (project terminology) for relatives and close associates of PEPs, whom Recommendation 12 also covers; FATF speaks of family members and close associates. Not re-checked against the June 2026 edition (FATF blocks automated access). | [fatf_recommendations_2026] [fatf_peps_2013] |
| Nominee | A person who formally holds shares or a directorship for someone else (the nominator) whose identity is not disclosed [fatf_egmont_concealment_2018]. Informal nominees include spouses, children, other relatives and associates [fatf_egmont_concealment_2018]. Europol describes nominee directors as "strawmen that appear on the paperwork, but do not operate independently and follow the instructions of the real owner" [europol_shadow_money_2021]. NeonRadar writes "nominee" and treats "strawman" and "proxy" as synonyms (project terminology). | [fatf_egmont_concealment_2018] [europol_shadow_money_2021] |
| Shell company | An "incorporated company with no independent operations, significant assets, ongoing business activities, or employees" [fatf_egmont_concealment_2018] (executive summary, p. 5). FinCEN notes that most shell companies are formed for legitimate purposes [fincen_fin_2006_g014]. The term describes a structure: never call a real, named company a shell. | [fatf_egmont_concealment_2018] [fincen_fin_2006_g014] |
| Shelf company | An "incorporated company with inactive shareholders, directors, and secretary" that "is left dormant for a longer period even if a customer relationship has already been established". | [fatf_egmont_concealment_2018] |
| Front company | A "fully functioning company with the characteristics of a legitimate business, serving to disguise and obscure illicit financial activity". The term describes a structure: never call a real, named company a front company. | [fatf_egmont_concealment_2018] |
| Trust and company service provider (TCSP) | A person or business that, for third parties, forms legal persons, acts as or arranges directors or secretaries, provides a registered office or address, acts as or arranges trustees, or acts as or arranges nominee shareholders. | [fatf_glossary] |
| DNFBP | Designated non-financial businesses and professions: casinos, real estate agents, dealers in precious metals and stones, lawyers, notaries, other independent legal professionals and accountants, and TCSPs. | [fatf_glossary] |
| STR and SAR | Suspicious transaction report (the FATF term, Recommendation 20) and suspicious activity report (the US term). NeonRadar treats them as equivalent and writes STR for FATF and non-US contexts and SAR for US material. | [fatf_recommendations_2026] [ffiec_appendix_f] [fincen_fin_2014_a005] |
| Financial intelligence unit (FIU) | The national centre that receives and analyses suspicious transaction reports and other relevant information and disseminates the results of its analysis. | [fatf_recommendations_2026] [egmont_fiu] |
| Structuring, smurfing | Structuring is splitting cash transactions into amounts below a reporting threshold to evade reporting requirements [cfr_31_1010_314] [ffiec_appendix_g]. "Smurfs" are "teams of persons" who structure transactions acting with or on behalf of other persons [fincen_fin_2025_a003]. | [cfr_31_1010_314] [ffiec_appendix_g] [fincen_fin_2025_a003] |
| Money mule | A person who receives money from a third party into their account and transfers it on [europol_money_muling]. Mules may act "wittingly or unwittingly" [fincen_fin_2025_a003]; FATF notes that they may be knowingly complicit, unwitting or negligent [fatf_pml_2018]. | [europol_money_muling] [fincen_fin_2025_a003] [fatf_pml_2018] |
| Funnel account | An account in one geographic area that receives multiple cash deposits, often below the cash reporting threshold, from which the funds are withdrawn in a different geographic area with little time elapsing between the deposits and the withdrawals. | [fincen_fin_2014_a005] |
| Pass-through or transit account | An account that passes funds on rather than holding them. The FATF and Egmont trade-based indicators speak of a "pay-through" or "transit" account [fatf_egmont_tbml_indicators_2021]; the indicators of concealed beneficial ownership describe a company whose "transactions appear to flow through the company in a short period of time with little other perceived purpose" [fatf_egmont_concealment_2018]. | [fatf_egmont_tbml_indicators_2021] [fatf_egmont_concealment_2018] |
| Mass registration address | An address listed against many companies, "usually the address of a TCSP that manages a number of shell companies on behalf of its customers". | [fatf_egmont_concealment_2018] |
| Round-tripping | Payments routed in a circle so that funds return to their origin. | [fatf_egmont_tbml_indicators_2021] |
| Trade-based money laundering (TBML) | Disguising the proceeds of crime and moving value through trade transactions, for example by misrepresenting the price, quantity or quality of goods. | [fatf_egmont_tbml_2020] |
| Correspondent banking | A business relationship in which one financial institution (the correspondent) provides commercial or business products or services to another bank (the respondent). | [wolfsberg_correspondent_2022] |
| Red flag, indicator | A feature that sources list as a reason to look closer; not evidence on its own. | [ffiec_appendix_f] |
| Structural pattern | NeonRadar's term for a graph shape that matches a typology's graph pattern. It describes data, not conduct. | project |
| Pattern strength | A number from 0 to 1 for how closely the evidence matches a typology's graph pattern. It orders the alert queue; it is never a risk or suspicion score. | project (C4 alert contract) |
| Synthetic scenario | A pattern planted by the generator among synthetic entities only. | project (guardrail G1) |
| Look-alike | Benign synthetic activity with the same shape as a typology (a refund cycle, a payroll fan-out), generated so that precision can be measured honestly. | project |
| Flag | A label from source data (`:Sanctioned`, `:PEP`, `:RCA`); never set by a detection. | project (C2 graph schema) |
| Alert, evidence subgraph | A C4 object produced by a detector, with the node and edge ids and the query that reproduce the finding. | project (C4 alert contract) |

## Entity resolution and ownership

Terms from Phase 2 ([PHASE-2.md](build/PHASE-2.md) 7.6). How NeonRadar uses them is explained in [docs/er/README.md](er/README.md).

| Term | Definition | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| Entity resolution | Deciding which records from one or more sources refer to the same real person or organisation, and merging them into one node. | project |
| Source record | One record as a source published it (a Companies House officer appointment, a sanctions list entry); `:SourceRecord` in the graph. | project (C2 graph schema) |
| Canonical entity | The node built from one or more source records; it has an `nr-` id when it was merged from two or more. | project |
| Possible match | Two nodes that may be the same entity but were not merged, because of the merge policy, a threshold or a source's own hint. Stored as `POSSIBLY_SAME_AS` and always described as "not confirmed"; deterministic and source-hint possible matches have no probability. A possible match never carries a flag, a topic or any other property from one node to the other. | project |
| Deterministic rule | A merge rule based on a shared official identifier (D1 to D6), such as a UK company number or an LEI. | project |
| Fellegi-Sunter model | The probabilistic record linkage model behind Splink: each comparison level has an m probability (how often it occurs among true matches) and a u probability (how often it occurs among non-matches), combined into a match weight [fellegi_sunter_1969] [splink_docs]. | [fellegi_sunter_1969] [splink_docs] |
| Blocking | Restricting comparisons to pairs of records that share some key, so that not every pair of records is compared [splink_docs]. | [splink_docs] |
| Match weight, match probability | The log-odds score of a pair (Splink uses base 2) and its conversion to a probability [splink_docs]. | [splink_docs] |
| Precision, recall | The share of merges that are correct; the share of true matches that were merged. | project |
| Evaluation run | The resolution run whose merges the published precision figure measures: the frozen configuration, with no review decisions applied ([PHASE-2.md](build/PHASE-2.md) 7.3). | project |
| Wilson interval | A confidence interval for a proportion that behaves well for small samples and for proportions near 0 or 1 [wilson_1927] [nist_handbook_7_2_4_1]. | [wilson_1927] [nist_handbook_7_2_4_1] |
| Cohen's kappa, positive and negative agreement | Measures of agreement between two labellers. Kappa corrects for chance agreement [cohen_1960]; the two specific agreements show agreement on `same` and on `different` separately, because kappa can be low when almost every label is the same [cicchetti_feinstein_1990]. | [cohen_1960] [cicchetti_feinstein_1990] |
| PSC | Person with significant control, recorded by Companies House. A corporate PSC is a company or other legal entity recorded in that role [ch_psc_snapshot]: in the Act's words a relevant legal entity, one that would count as a person with significant control if it were an individual and that is subject to its own disclosure requirements [ca_2006_s790c]. Each interest is recorded as a nature of control with a statutory band, for example "more than 25% but not more than 50%" of the shares [psc_regulations_2016_sch2] [ch_psc_descriptions]. | [ch_psc_snapshot] [ca_2006_s790c] [psc_regulations_2016_sch2] [ch_psc_descriptions] |
| Effective share | The product of the published bands along one ownership chain, as a range: a NeonRadar calculation that shows how bands combine. Sanctions ownership tests do not multiply shares down a chain: OFAC passes blocked status entity by entity [ofac_50_percent_faqs]. A band on the PSC register may already be an indirect holding [ca_2006_sch1a_para9], so multiplying it again can understate it. | [ofac_50_percent_faqs] [ca_2006_sch1a_para9] |
| Consolidation (GLEIF) | Accounting consolidation of a child by a parent, as reported to GLEIF; not an ownership percentage [gleif_level2]. | [gleif_level2] |
| Flag topic | A topic that is not purely descriptive, such as `sanction`, `role.pep` or `crime`; flags on nodes come only from these, and only from a node's own source records ([PHASE-2.md](build/PHASE-2.md) 7.1.1). | project |
| Listed record | A record with at least one flag topic: on a sanctions, PEP or similar list ([PHASE-2.md](build/PHASE-2.md) 7.1.1). | project |
