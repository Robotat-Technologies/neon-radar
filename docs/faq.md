# Frequently asked questions

Short answers to the questions people ask first, with links to the page that says it in full. If an answer here and the linked page ever disagree, the linked page is right: tell us and we will fix this one.

## Does NeonRadar accuse anyone?

No. NeonRadar finds structures in a graph, such as money that leaves an account and comes back through a chain of transfers, and shows them to a person to judge. A match is a reason to look, not a finding. The standing disclaimer, shown at the top of the [README](../README.md) and on every interface page with entities or alerts, says it plainly: "A pattern match is not evidence of money laundering, corruption or any other wrongdoing."

How the project keeps to this:

- **Fixed wording.** Text next to an entity describes the pattern ("funds returned to the originating account through 4 transfers") and never labels the entity. The words it may and may not use, and why, are in [wording.md](wording.md); a lint checks the templates against the forbidden list.
- **Pattern strength, not a risk score.** An alert's number says how closely the evidence matches a typology's graph pattern. It is not the probability of anything ([wording.md](wording.md), "Vocabulary for entity-facing text").
- **Legitimate look-alikes are listed.** Every typology explainer has a "false positives" section, for example a loan paid out and paid back for the circular-flow pattern ([typologies/](typologies/README.md)).
- **Flags only from sources.** A "sanctioned" or "politically exposed person" flag appears only when a source list published it for that record; NeonRadar never infers one (guardrail G8 in [build/PHASE-2.md](build/PHASE-2.md)).
- **The planned assistant refuses.** The question-answering assistant, planned for Phase 4, refuses questions about guilt, risk, decisions or predictions rather than guessing (guardrail G14 in [build/PHASE-4.md](build/PHASE-4.md)).

## Can I use it on real people?

Not in the demo, and only with care anywhere else.

- **The demo is synthetic.** Every company, person, address, account and payment in `make demo-mini` was generated by NeonRadar, and none of them exists; their ids start with `syn-` ([quickstart.md](quickstart.md), [DATA_SOURCES.md](../DATA_SOURCES.md)).
- **Patterns are planted only among synthetic entities.** No real person or company is ever given a role in a planted scenario. Real register entries may receive only ordinary synthetic background activity, and any exception needs a decision record approved by the project lead and the data steward (guardrail G1 in [build/PHASE-1.md](build/PHASE-1.md)).
- **No alert names a real person.** Every payment in NeonRadar is synthetic, so a payment-based finding that involves a real entity is withheld and only counted, and so is any finding whose subject or evidence is a real person (guardrail G12 in [build/PHASE-3.md](build/PHASE-3.md)).
- **Real register data stays on approved machines.** The loaders can fetch public registers, which contain real people (company officers, people on sanctions lists). That data stays under `data/cache/real/` and in a separate Neo4j instance, on a machine the data steward approved, and is purged after a set number of days (guardrail G9 in [build/PHASE-2.md](build/PHASE-2.md)). The project currently runs on synthetic data only: no real-data profile is enabled until the data protection documents are filed and approved machines are listed ([operator decision OD-22](build/operator-decisions.md)).
- **If you load real data yourself, the responsibility is yours.** NeonRadar's position is that whoever loads personal data for their own purposes is normally a controller under UK or EU data protection law and needs their own lawful basis; whether that holds in a given setting is for that user to establish ([DATA_SOURCES.md](../DATA_SOURCES.md), which is not legal advice). Whenever any loaded dataset is not synthetic, the interface shows the real data notice ([wording.md](wording.md)).
- **Screenshots and recordings show synthetic data only.** Check the [screenshot and video policy](screenshot-policy.md) before you publish one.

## Where does the data come from?

From two places, and the repository ships only the first:

- **NeonRadar's own generator.** The demo and the test fixtures are synthetic records made by NeonRadar's generator (`nr_synthetic`, Apache-2.0). The generator is deterministic: the same seed, with the same code and dependency versions, gives the same records ([build/PHASE-1.md](build/PHASE-1.md), M1.8e).
- **Public sources, fetched by you.** NeonRadar ships loaders, never data. The loaders fetch public registers and lists into a local cache that is never committed: UK Companies House, the GLEIF index, the ICIJ Offshore Leaks Database and the UK, US (OFAC) and EU sanctions lists. Three more have conditions: the UN sanctions list only with the UN's written permission, UK Parliament members only when the project enables PEP sources, and OpenSanctions only after a licence decision. Each source's licence, attribution text, commercial-use terms and personal-data notes are in [DATA_SOURCES.md](../DATA_SOURCES.md), and the reasons for this design are in [ADR-0006](decisions/0006-data-licensing.md).

In the interface, the Data sources page lists every dataset with its licence and notice, and how many records were loaded from it. NeonRadar never publishes a database that merges sources, and attribution travels with the data into screenshots, exports and reports.

## Does NeonRadar send my data anywhere?

No. It runs on your machine and phones home to nobody: no telemetry, no analytics and no outbound calls, except the data loaders you run yourself ([README](../README.md), "What it is"). The demo listens on `127.0.0.1` only ([quickstart.md](quickstart.md)).

## Is NeonRadar a compliance tool?

No. It is an educational, open-source reference project. It is not regulated or certified, and it does not make anyone compliant with any law or regulation ([README](../README.md), "What it is not"). Detection results measured on synthetic data say nothing about how it would perform on real data.
